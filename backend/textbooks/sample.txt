Deep learning is a subset of machine learning that is based on artificial neural networks with multiple layers, also known as deep neural networks. These architectures are inspired by the structure and function of the human brain, specifically the interconnected web of neurons. 

One of the most common types of deep neural networks used for image recognition and computer vision tasks is the Convolutional Neural Network (CNN). A CNN typically consists of convolutional layers, pooling layers, and fully connected layers. The convolutional layers apply filters to the input image to extract features like edges and textures, while pooling layers reduce the spatial dimensions to decrease computational load and control overfitting. Regularization techniques, such as Dropout (which randomly deactivates neurons during training) and L2 Regularization, are often employed to force the network to learn more robust features rather than relying on specific pathways.

Another important architecture is the Recurrent Neural Network (RNN), which is uniquely designed to handle sequential data such as text, audio, or time series by maintaining a 'hidden state' that captures memory of previous inputs. However, traditional RNNs struggle with retaining long-term information due to the vanishing gradient problem. A powerful variation of RNNs, called Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks, was specifically developed with 'gates' (forget, input, and output gates) to solve this problem, enabling the network to learn long-term dependencies in the data over thousands of time steps without degrading the gradient.
